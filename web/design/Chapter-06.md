## 1. Purpose and story arc

**The problem.** A TurtleBot3 in a room it has never seen has 5 Hz, 360-beam lidar scans and wheel odometry, and nothing else. It has to produce a trajectory and a map, and each is an input to the other. The measurable form
(the repository's claims, graded by a script whose limits were written before the first run): *the trajectory of the keyframes against the ground truth, with and without loop closure (S4), and the number of loop constraints the robot accepted that were wrong, pre-registered to be zero (S5)*.

**Arc.** *Problem → what we try first and why it fails → the idea → the Rust → the number again.* The first thing to try is to chain scan matches (ICP of each scan against the recent past) and call the chain the trajectory. On this robot, in this room, it is *already good*: 3.5 mm rms on the simulated run, 24 mm on the Webots one
(w6.1 opens on it). That is a finding, and a surprise for a chapter about drift: the repository's evaluation had to inject a systematic yaw error to make the chain drift, and it says so. With the error injected, the chain is 24 cm off, the scans drawn on it smear into double walls, and the pose graph, holding a few hundred
loop constraints between keyframes that see the same place, pulls the trajectory back to 1 cm. And with *no* error injected the graph is not better: on the simulated run it is a little worse (2.53 times the chain's error), and the repository's pre-registered check says FAIL. The idea is the graph (w6.1, in the problem, shows all of this running);
what it rests on is scan matching (w6.2: ICP converging, and not converging, from a bad start) and, above all, on **not believing every loop**: a false loop closure bends the map into a shape that is self-consistent and wrong, so every candidate is verified before it is believed, by a stricter standard than the one that found it (w6.3: a candidate that fits to 2 cm and is refused, then
accepted when a box is added to the room). The part that makes this chapter a chapter of *Probabilistic Robotics* and not a recipe: **the number that says the verifier is safe depends on what "wrong" is measured against.** Graded against the pose at the time stamp, the verifier accepted 54 wrong constraints of 164;
graded against the pose at the instant the beams were measured, none of 164, *with the same decisions*. The chapter shows how that was found, what independent evidence says which labelling is right (the exact room explains a turning scan to 17 mm at the measured instant and to 53 mm at the stamp), and what it does not prove.

**Epigraph.** "Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) consists in the concurrent construction of a model of the environment (the map), and the estimation of the state of the robot moving within it." C. Cadena et al., *Past, Present, and Future of Simultaneous Localization and Mapping: Towards the Robust-Perception Age* (IEEE Transactions on Robotics 32(6), 2016), first sentence of the abstract
(checked against the arXiv record 1606.05830; the arXiv text has no space after "(SLAM)").

## 3. Foundation

Statement first.

1. **The problem as a posterior.** `p(x_{1:t}, m | z_{1:t}, u_{1:t})`; graph SLAM approximates it by the maximum of the trajectory posterior given relative-pose constraints; the map is rendered afterwards from keyframe scans (a statement of what the repository does, not a theorem).
2. **A relative-pose constraint.** For keyframes `i`, `j` and a measurement `Z` of `X_i⁻¹ X_j`, the residual is `e = log(Z⁻¹ X_i⁻¹ X_j)` and the cost `eᵀ Λ e`, with `Λ` the information of the constraint; its Jacobians for right perturbations are `∂e/∂δ_j = J_r⁻¹(e)` and `∂e/∂δ_i = −J_r⁻¹(e) Ad_{T⁻¹}` (`geom::relative_pose_residual`; the crate's tests check them against central differences).
3. **The optimizer.** Gauss–Newton solves `(H) δ = −b`, `H = Σ Jᵀ Λ J`; Levenberg–Marquardt solves `(H + λ diag H) δ = −b` and accepts the step if the cost falls, with `λ` updated by the gain ratio (the code is printed). A gauge prior on the first keyframe makes `H` positive definite. Robust kernels (a Huber kernel on loop edges) limit the pull of a constraint.
4. **ICP.** Point-to-plane: minimize `Σ w (n·(T p − q))²` over `T`; weights Geman–McClure with scale `τ/3`; a correspondence threshold `τ` that adapts to the front end's own prediction error. The information of the result is `Λ = (1/σ²) Σ w JᵀJ / info_inflation` and is **overconfident** (the beams are not independent): measured by S2 (mean NEES 3.14 on the simulated run, against a chi-squared(3) mean of 3; on Webots it depends on the labels, 32.0 against 1.06).
5. **Why a chain drifts, and a graph does not (when it has loops).** Each increment has an error; they compound; a heading error becomes a position error that grows with distance. A loop constraint says two far-apart keyframes are close; the optimizer spreads the contradiction over the chain. **Worked example 1** (pinned by the checks): the injected error of 0.004 rad per keyframe takes the chain to 242.5 mm rms and the graph to 9.8 mm (ratio 0.040); with no injection the chain is 3.5 mm and the graph 9.0 mm (ratio 2.53, over the limit 1.05).
6. **The verifier: four checks after the fit.** (a) overlap, (b) ICP settles, (c) the fit explains at least half the scan's points within 5 cm and at least 80 of them, (d) not degenerate, (e) consistent: `ν = log(Z_icp⁻¹ T_pred)`, `S = Σ_rel + Σ_icp`, `νᵀ S⁻¹ ν ≤ χ²(3, 0.95)`, (f) unambiguous: restarts (±0.3 m, ±0.25 rad, the quarter turns and the half turn) that settle on another good fit explaining at least the main fit's share less 5 % of the points make it an alias. The χ² gate **cannot** reject a false candidate that lies inside the graph's own uncertainty; (d) and (f) exist for those. **Worked example 2:** the χ² against the believed offset in the pillar room (0.10 m: 4.12, accepted; 0.15 m: 9.18, rejected), gate 7.775.
   **A finding about the crate:** the gate is `chi2_quantile(3, 0.95)` computed by the Wilson–Hilferty approximation, 7.775, where the exact value is 7.815 (0.5 % lower, so the gate is a hair stricter). Nothing depends on it; the page shows 7.775 because that is the number the verifier compares with.
7. **What a label is.** Claim S5 counts accepted constraints whose measurement differs from the *ground-truth relative pose* of the two scans by more than 0.05 m or 0.03 rad. The ground truth of a scan is the track at the instant the beams were measured. The Webots lidar measures on a regular 0.2 s lattice and its stamp lags the measurement by up to 0.164 s (0.061 s on average), so the track at `stamp − 0.06 s` is wrong in a turn at 0.8 rad/s by up to about 0.14 rad, more than S5's own tolerance. An estimate of the measured instant from the scans' *ranges* against the exact room (no SLAM output), calibrated on the first three turns and checked on the other ten, differs by 0.4 ms between the two sets.
8. **Two other back ends, briefly (S7, informative, non-blocking).** EKF-SLAM keeps robot and landmarks in one Gaussian and cannot revise an old decision; the grid RBPF keeps a particle per trajectory hypothesis with its own grid. On the Rusty rectangle run with a *declared* synthetic odometry noise: dead reckoning 122.2 mm rms, EKF-SLAM 12.8 mm, RBPF with 30 particles and no scan-matching proposal 2.42 m. No ranking is claimed: the odometry noise is a setting of the simulator. (A short section, no widget.)

## 4. Conceptual

In the order the reader meets them.

1. **w6.1, "From scans to a trajectory: the chain, the graph and the loops"** (in *The problem*). A recorded run of the rectangle route, three laps, processed scan by scan by the repository's pipeline (compiled to WebAssembly): the orange chain (ICP alone), the purple optimized graph, green lines for accepted loop constraints, the ground truth dashed gray. Autoplays (about 20 s). Foregrounded: the yaw error injected into every odometry edge (0 to 0.004 rad per keyframe); a toggle draws the scans at the chain's poses instead of the graph's; for the Webots recording a second control chooses the ground truth the constraints are graded against.
   Kills **two** misconceptions, both named in `teaches`: "closing a loop always makes the trajectory better" (at 0 rad the graph is worse than the chain on the simulated run, and the dashboard shows both) and "the ground truth of a scan is just the pose at its time stamp" (on the Webots recording, the wrong-constraint count is 54 with the stamp labels and 0 with the measured-instant ones, with the verifier's decisions identical).
2. **w6.2, "Two scans, one pose: ICP from a wrong start"** (in *Building intuition*). Two recorded scans one second apart (the pair claim S1b uses), the second placed by ICP's pose after iteration `k` from a start the reader sets: rotation error, an offset along x, point-to-plane or point-to-point; the residual per iteration; the true pose as a gray glyph. Autoplays through the iterations. Foregrounded: the starting rotation error.
   Kills "scan matching always finds the alignment, whatever the first guess": from 0.5 rad point-to-plane lands (28 iterations), from 0.6 it has not settled when its 50 iterations are over, from 0.7 it settles 31 cm and 0.71 rad away with a residual of 140 mm against 21 mm; a start 1 m ahead settles 0.95 m off with a residual of 50 mm. The repository's S1b over random starts: 100 of 100 up to (0.5 m, 0.4 rad), 40 of 100 at (1 m, 0.8 rad) (asserted).
3. **w6.3, "A loop that fits is not a loop that is true"** (in the section on loop closure). One loop candidate between two views of a seeded room: scans overlaid at where the graph believed the second view was, where ICP puts it and the truth; the verifier's six checks listed with a mark each; the residual, χ² and gate. Static (informative state at load): the empty square room at a quarter turn, rejected as ambiguous. Foregrounded: how far off the graph believes the second view is (the gate); rooms and the rotation are secondary controls; a re-roll changes the lidar noise.
   Kills "if ICP lines two scans up with a small residual, the robot has been to this place before": the residual is 17 to 22 mm for the alias (rejected), the true loop (accepted) and the loop the graph is sure is elsewhere (rejected at the gate) alike (asserted).
   **Decision: is a third widget worth its place? Yes**, because the verifier is the part of the chapter that makes the claim S5 safe, its four checks are not visible in a figure, and the empty-room alias (a rotation of the whole scan) is a property of *angle*, which a drag shows.
   **The hall of the repository's tests is not offered:** whether it is refused as degenerate or as not converged depends on the noise realization, and the realization on the page differs from the robot's (TOC §5).

**Findings from compiling the algorithm crates (TOC §5).** `robotics_slam` compiles to `wasm32-unknown-unknown` **with no source change**, sparse `faer` solver included. The one thing to say is that the module's replay of a recorded stream equals the native harness's output (trajectory errors to better than 1e-12 m, loop-closure counts exactly) on four streams, although ICP, the optimizer and the verifier run on a different floating-point library: the replay has no random stream, so the seed divergence of TOC §5 does not touch it. The two recordings and the simulated one are not compiled in (1.9 MB and two of 2.5 MB): `npm run wasm` copies them from `evidence/` to `public/data/` and a check asserts each copy. The bindings are in the book's single module (`crates/robotics_wasm/src/ch06.rs`), not in a separate `lib/wasm/slam/` bundle, for the reason Chapter 4 gives (one loader, one download).
**Cost on the page:** the replay of 844 to 894 scans takes 11 to 13 s in Node on the module (14 ms a scan, with 0.25 s for the worst step, a keyframe that verifies loops); the widget stepping four scans a frame at 10 frames a second shows it in about 25 s. The verifier widget recomputes in about 40 ms.

## 7. Exercises

1. **F, difficulty 2, "The residual of a loop edge".** For two keyframes `X_i`, `X_j` and a measurement `Z`, show that `e = log(Z⁻¹ X_i⁻¹ X_j)` is zero exactly when the graph agrees with the measurement, and that for a pure translation measurement along x between two poses facing east it reduces to the difference of the x coordinates less the measurement. *Sketch:* `E = Z⁻¹T` is the identity iff `T = Z`; for rotations zero, `exp` is addition.
2. **F, difficulty 3, "Why a clean fit proves nothing about the alias".** Take the empty square room and a lidar at its centre. Show that the scan taken facing east and the one facing north are the same, so that ICP started from the true pose and from the quarter turn reach the same residual; say what the verifier's restarts compare and why the margin of 5 % of the points exists. *Sketch:* the room is invariant under a quarter turn about its centre; read `verify`'s ambiguity block and `LoopConfig::ambiguity_margin`.
3. **C, difficulty 1, "No error, no gain".** Predict, before touching the slider of w6.1 on the simulated data, whether the optimized graph at 0 rad of injected error is better than the chain. *Answer:* no; the chain is 3.5 mm and the graph 9.0 mm, because the scan matcher drifts by millimetres and the graph spreads the (small, correct) loop constraints' own errors; this is the repository's failed S4 ratio check on every natural simulated run.
4. **C, difficulty 2, "Where does ICP stop".** Predict where point-to-plane ICP ends from a start 1 m ahead of the truth (w6.2) and what its own output says about it. *Answer:* settled about 0.95 m off, with a residual of 50 mm: the only sign is the residual.
5. **C, difficulty 2, "Add a box".** Predict what happens to w6.3 at 90° when the box is added to the empty room. *Answer:* accepted; the box breaks the symmetry, so the restarts that settle elsewhere explain less of the scan than the main fit by more than the 5 % margin.
6. **P, difficulty 2, "The hall".** In `crates/robotics_wasm/src/ch06.rs` add the repository's 2 m wide, 40 m long hall (`corridor` in `crates/robotics_slam/tests/common/mod.rs`) as room 3 of `ch06_verify`, and run it for several seeds. Which reason do you get, and does it depend on the seed? *Answer:* refused in every case, as not converged or as degenerate depending on the noise realization; this is why it is not offered in w6.3.

## 8. Modernization notes

* **Against the 2005 text.** *Probabilistic Robotics* presents SLAM as EKF-SLAM, GraphSLAM and FastSLAM (Chapters 10, 11 and 13). The crate's main line is graph SLAM: a factor graph, a Levenberg–Marquardt optimizer on SE(2) with exact right Jacobians, point-to-plane ICP with an adaptive correspondence threshold in the manner of KISS-ICP, and a loop verifier with checks beyond a fit and a χ² gate (the repository's decision DS16 lists where it departs from the reference chapters of *Probabilistic Robotics via Rust*, among them one more check: ambiguity under restarts and quarter turns). EKF-SLAM and the grid RBPF are kept as comparison back ends.
* **Limitations carried from the repository, repeated and not hidden.**
  * **S5 is met on one labelling.** With the recorded labels it fails on all three Webots gradings (54, 63 and 54 wrong); with the measured-instant labels it passes (0 of 164 offline, 0 of 156 on the live nodes' own loops). The roadmap closed the part with that in its heading. The chapter does not call this a clean pass of the criterion as first written.
  * **The timing offset of the lattice differs between runs.** The Phase 4 probe's run had a different alignment of the lattice against the ground-truth clock (0.0603 s) from the three Phase 5 runs (0.001 to 0.002 s); why is not known. An earlier comparison of the two was a comparison between runs, not a measurement mismatch; the label estimate is therefore made per run and checked per run.
  * **The pass is about the candidates the run produced:** 182 candidates, 18 of them rejected at the χ² gate, none for any other reason; the candidates designed to be rejected are in the repository's tests, not in Webots data. Zero wrong in 164 supports a wrong-accept rate below about 1.8 % at 95 % confidence, in this world.
  * **S4 on simulated data fails its pre-registered ratio on all four natural runs** (there is no drift to repair); on the Webots run the graph is 0.54 to 0.72 of the chain's error.
  * **One room, one route, one robot, simulated;** the nodes ran live against Webots twice; loop closure is opt-in in the node; the checker for the nodes' shutdown has a stationary-odometry assumption and the node drops its first `map → odom` at start-up in some runs.
  * **S8 is not claimable**, and place recognition, landmark FastSLAM and the TSDF are not built.
* **The benchmark has no external comparison.** There is no Python port and no Cartographer, slam_toolbox or other reference stack in this repository's evidence for SLAM; the chapter prints no such number and no placeholder.
