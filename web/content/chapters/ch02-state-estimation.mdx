Chapter 1 ended with a robot that integrates its wheels exactly and keeps a distribution over where
it is. Drive that robot for three minutes and two things go wrong at once. The pose is wrong, by
metres, because a wheel is a little larger than its twin and the floor is a little slippery. And the
robot does not know it is wrong: the covariance that comes with its odometry stays small.

A gyro is the obvious second opinion, and the second half of the problem is that it is an opinion
with its own error, a slowly wandering bias. This chapter builds the filter that lets each sensor
correct the other, then does what a filter's author owes the reader: it asks whether the
filter's *own* idea of its error is right. That question has a precise form (NEES and NIS), and it
turns out that the answer depends on decisions the equations do not contain.

</Overview>

## The problem

Rusty is the repository's headless differential-drive simulator (Chapter 3 opens it up). Give it the
mixed 180 s trajectory of the repository's estimation tests (straight runs, turns in place, arcs,
a spin across ±π, stops) and the three imperfections a real TurtleBot3 has: 2 % random slip per
wheel per step, a right wheel whose radius is 0.2 % too large, and a gyro with a bias of about
0.005 rad/s that drifts. Then ask two questions of the raw wheel odometry, one about accuracy and
one about honesty.

On the repository's recorded run (seed 1), the raw odometry ends with a position RMSE of 0.710 m
and a heading RMSE of 0.2564 rad. Its own covariance claims far better: the mean *normalized estimation
error squared* of its pose is 185.24, where a filter whose covariance describes its error averages 3.
After fusing the gyro, the same run gives 0.152 m and 0.0476 rad, a mean NEES of 0.45 and a mean
*normalized innovation squared* of 0.90 where 2 is expected (`docs/STATE_ESTIMATION.md`, §4.1).
Better, and no longer lying: if anything, too modest.

## Building intuition

If the wheels drift, listen to something else. A gyro measures the heading's rate directly, with no
wheel and no floor in the way. Integrate it and you have a heading that does not care about slip.
Try it.

<GyroAlone />

Two things stand out. With the bias set to zero the gyro alone is usually the best of the three, better than the wheels
and than the fused filter, which is what a sensor that measures rotation directly should be. But a real gyro has
a bias, a small constant rate it reports when the robot is not turning, and integrating a rate
integrates its bias with it: 0.005 rad/s held for 180 s is 0.9 rad. The error of the gyro alone
grows linearly in time, in proportion to the bias you drag in, while the fused filter's error barely
moves. It does not ignore the bias; it *measures* it, by noticing that the wheels and the gyro
disagree about a robot that is standing still or turning in place. The chapter is the arithmetic of
that sentence.

## The mathematics

### Why an error state

A pose has three numbers and one of them wraps. A Kalman filter wants a state in a vector space,
and a Gaussian over `(x, y, θ)` is wrong near ±π and wrong again because the motion is not linear in
those coordinates. The way out is the one Chapter 1's `⊞` already provides. Keep a *nominal* state
that lives on the manifold and is integrated exactly, and keep a Gaussian only over the small
*error* between the truth and the nominal state, which lives in the tangent space, a plain vector
space where the Gaussian is a fine description. This is the error-state Kalman filter
(Solà, 2017), here on SE(2).

The nominal state is the pose, the forward speed and the gyro bias:

$$
\hat x = (\hat T,\, \hat v,\, \hat b),\qquad \hat T \in \SEtwo .
$$

The error is five numbers, with the position error in the body frame of the estimate (the *right*
perturbation, Chapter 1):

$$
T = \hat T \,\exp(\delta\xi),\quad v = \hat v + \delta v,\quad b = \hat b + \delta b,\qquad
\delta x = (\delta\rho_x,\, \delta\rho_y,\, \delta\theta,\, \delta v,\, \delta b) \in \R^5 ,
$$

with covariance $P \in \R^{5\times 5}$. Everything the filter knows about its own uncertainty is
that matrix.

### Process: integrate the gyro, propagate the error

Between measurements the gyro rate $\omega_m$ is held constant, the bias estimate is subtracted,
and the nominal pose takes the exact step of Chapter 1:

$$
\omega = \omega_m - \hat b,\qquad \tau = (\hat v\,\Delta t,\; 0,\; \omega\,\Delta t),\qquad \hat T \leftarrow \hat T \bplus \tau .
$$

The error moves by a matrix $F$ that the derivation below produces, and the covariance by
$P \leftarrow F P F^{\T} + Q$:

$$
F = \begin{pmatrix} \Ad(\exp\tau)^{-1} & \Delta t\, e_x & -\Delta t\, e_\theta \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Q = \diag(\sigma_{\text{lat}}^2,\ \sigma_{\text{lat}}^2,\ \sigma_g^2,\ \sigma_a^2,\ \sigma_{bw}^2)\,\Delta t .
$$

Read the columns. A speed error $\delta v$ becomes a position error along the body $x$ axis
($\Delta t\, e_x$). A bias error $\delta b$ becomes a heading error that grows by $-\Delta t$ per step,
because $\omega = \omega_m - \hat b - \delta b$. The block $\Ad(\exp\tau)^{-1}$ carries last step's pose
error into the frame of the new estimate. The noise densities are the gyro's white noise $\sigma_g$, a
random walk of the speed $\sigma_a$ and of the bias $\sigma_{bw}$, and a small planar slip diffusion
$\sigma_{\text{lat}}$ that keeps unmodeled slip from making the filter over-confident.

<Derivation title="Where F comes from" result="δξ⁺ ≈ Ad(exp τ̂)⁻¹ δξ + δτ, and δτ = (δv Δt, 0, −δb Δt)">

The true pose after the step is $T\,\exp(\tau_{\text{true}})$ and the nominal one is $\hat T\,\exp(\hat\tau)$,
with $\tau_{\text{true}} = \hat\tau + \delta\tau$. The new error $\delta\xi^{+}$ is defined by
$T\exp(\tau_{\text{true}}) = \hat T\exp(\hat\tau)\exp(\delta\xi^{+})$, so, with $T = \hat T\exp(\delta\xi)$,

$$
\exp(\delta\xi^{+}) = \exp(\hat\tau)^{-1}\exp(\delta\xi)\exp(\hat\tau)\;\exp(\delta\tau)
\;=\; \exp\bigl(\Ad(\exp\hat\tau)^{-1}\delta\xi\bigr)\exp(\delta\tau),
$$

using the identity $g^{-1}\exp(\eta)\,g = \exp(\Ad(g^{-1})\eta)$ with $g = \exp\hat\tau$, and
where $\exp(\delta\tau)$ stands for $\exp(\hat\tau)^{-1}\exp(\hat\tau + \delta\tau)$, which equals
$\exp(J_r(\hat\tau)\,\delta\tau)$ to first order. For the short steps between samples the right
Jacobian $J_r \approx I$, so to first order $\delta\xi^{+} \approx \Ad(\exp\hat\tau)^{-1}\delta\xi + \delta\tau$.
The twist error comes from the speed and the rate: $\delta\tau = (\delta v\,\Delta t,\; 0,\; -\delta b\,\Delta t)$ plus noise.
The two non-trivial columns of $F$ are exactly these.

</Derivation>

### Measurement: the wheels and the gyro disagree about the bias

The wheel odometry reports a forward speed $v_w$ and a yaw rate $\omega_w$ over the last interval;
the gyro reports $\omega_g = \omega + b + \text{noise}$. Take $\omega_w \approx \omega$, the true rate, and form

$$
z = (v_w,\; \omega_w - \omega_g),\qquad h(x) = (v,\; -b),\qquad
H = \begin{pmatrix} 0&0&0&1&0 \\ 0&0&0&0&-1 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
R = \diag\bigl(\sigma_v^2,\ \sigma_{\omega w}^2 + \sigma_{\omega g}^2\bigr).
$$

The first row observes the speed. The second observes *minus the bias*: if the wheels say the robot
turns at the rate the gyro reports minus 0.005 rad/s, the bias is 0.005 rad/s. The update is
Chapter 1's Joseph form on this $H$,

$$
S = H P H^{\T} + R,\qquad K = P H^{\T} S^{-1},\qquad
P \leftarrow (I - KH)\,P\,(I - KH)^{\T} + K R K^{\T},
$$

with the innovation $r = z - h(\hat x)$ and the correction $\delta\hat x = K r$.

### Injection and reset

The correction is applied to the nominal state, $\hat x \leftarrow \hat x \bplus \delta\hat x$. After
that the error is, by definition, measured from the *new* nominal state, so the covariance, which
was the covariance of the error from the old one, has to be re-expressed:

$$
P \leftarrow G\,P\,G^{\T},\qquad G = \diag\bigl(I_3 - \tfrac12\,\mathrm{ad}(\delta\hat\xi),\ 1,\ 1\bigr),
$$

where $\mathrm{ad}$ is the Lie-algebra adjoint of $\mathfrak{se}(2)$. Many implementations skip it
($G = I$) and call the error "small enough". The repository applies it, and keeps a switch to turn it
off so a test can show the difference.

<Derivation title="Where G comes from" result="δξ⁺ ≈ (I − ½ ad(δ̂ξ))(δξ − δ̂ξ)">

Before injection the truth is $\hat T\exp(\delta\xi)$. After it the nominal pose is $\hat T^{+} = \hat T\exp(\hat\delta)$, with
$\hat\delta$ the translational-and-heading part of the correction, and the new error satisfies
$\hat T\exp(\delta\xi) = \hat T^{+}\exp(\delta\xi^{+})$, so $\exp(\delta\xi^{+}) = \exp(-\hat\delta)\exp(\delta\xi)$.
The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula, to first order in the small quantities, gives
$\delta\xi^{+} \approx \delta\xi - \hat\delta - \tfrac12[\hat\delta, \delta\xi]$. The residual $\delta\xi - \hat\delta$ is
small and $[\hat\delta,\hat\delta] = 0$, so $[\hat\delta, \delta\xi] = [\hat\delta, \delta\xi - \hat\delta]$ and

$$
\delta\xi^{+} \approx \bigl(I - \tfrac12\,\mathrm{ad}(\hat\delta)\bigr)(\delta\xi - \hat\delta).
$$

The speed and bias components add, so their block of $G$ is the identity. The mean of $\delta\xi - \hat\delta$ is zero, and
its covariance is the corrected $P$; mapping it through $I - \tfrac12\mathrm{ad}(\hat\delta)$ gives $G P G^{\T}$.

</Derivation>

## Does the answer deserve trust?

A filter produces a mean and a covariance. The mean is an estimate; the covariance is a
*statement about the mean*: "my error is distributed like $\mathcal N(0, P)$". Statements can be
tested.

If the true error $e$ of an estimate has covariance $P$, then

$$
\varepsilon = e^{\T} P^{-1} e \;\sim\; \chi^2_N,\qquad \E[\varepsilon] = N,
$$

for an $N$-dimensional error. This is the **NEES**, the normalized estimation error squared. It needs
the truth, so it exists only in simulation. Its sibling needs no truth: the *innovation* $r = z - h(\hat x)$ of each update has covariance $S$, so

$$
\nu = r^{\T} S^{-1} r \;\sim\; \chi^2_M,\qquad \E[\nu] = M ,
$$

the **NIS**, which the robot can compute about itself while it drives. The repository's update
returns it with every correction.

A single sample is noisy; the time average of $n$ samples of a $\chi^2_N$ statistic is
$\chi^2_{nN}/n$, which concentrates around $N$. The filter passes the test at level $\alpha$ when the
mean lies in the interval $\bigl[\chi^2_{nN}(\alpha/2)/n,\ \chi^2_{nN}(1-\alpha/2)/n\bigr]$. Two
numbers pin the quantiles: $\chi^2_1(0.95) = 3.841459$ and $\chi^2_2(0.95) = 5.991465$, both reproduced by
the repository's tests and by this book's checks. For the 9000 pose samples of a 180 s drive the
95 % band for the mean is about $[2.95,\ 3.05]$, very narrow, which is why a filter that is only a
little off fails it. One caveat belongs next to the test: successive samples of a running filter
are correlated, the interval assumes they are not, and so a result near the edge is
indicative, not a verdict.

Which of the three outcomes a filter gets matters more than the pass or fail. Above the band, the
covariance is too small: the filter is **over-confident**, which is the dangerous direction, because
whatever consumes the estimate will trust it. Below the band it is **conservative**: safe, and
wasteful. Inside, it is consistent. Now see all three.

<Trust />

Three things to take from the dashboard. First, the raw odometry's mean NEES is over 100 against
an expected 3 at the documented radius error, and moving the slider shows the reason: the repository's raw covariance
(Chapter 1's `OdometryNoise`) models neither slip nor a radius mismatch, so it is an honest covariance
for a robot that does not exist. Second, the fused filter is *conservative* on every seed, and the repository
means it to be: real wheels slip more than a simulator's white noise, so its wheel noise is left wide.
Third, consistency is a property of the model against the world, not of the algorithm. The fused filter is
the same code at every slider position, and toward a 2 % radius error it leaves the band (on every one of twelve seeds the build tries at 2 %,
on about half of them at 1 %), because nothing in its model says that a wheel can be that wrong.

## Two decisions the equations do not make

### When may the filter correct the gyro bias?

The second row of $z$ is the wheel yaw rate minus the gyro's. It measures $-b$ only if $\omega_w$ is
the true rate. The reason it is not is that a wheel radius error makes
$\omega_w$ wrong *in proportion to the motion*, and it does so for minutes at a time, an error
no white noise can describe. Let the filter treat that difference as a measurement of the bias while the
robot drives and it will do the only thing it can: move the bias estimate until the difference stops
looking wrong. The bias estimate absorbs the radius error, and the heading inherits it.

The repository's answer is a policy, not a term in a matrix. The two-row update is used only while the
forward speed is at most `bias_update_max_speed`, 0.05 m/s: at standstill and in a turn in place, where
the wheels' yaw rate is the trustworthy one (a zero-velocity update). Above it, the update observes the speed alone.

<BiasPolicy />

The repository's author measured the same effect on the recorded native run, and the document says
it in one sentence: updating the bias at every speed raises the fused error to 0.62–0.79 m and
0.19–0.27 rad over the five seeds, and drops the yaw ±3σ coverage to 42–89 %
(`docs/STATE_ESTIMATION.md` §4.1; `cargo test` asserts that sentence). The page's own seeds agree in
direction on all five, which the build also asserts. The lesson generalizes: when an error is
correlated in time, a filter that models it as white noise is not merely sub-optimal, it is
confidently wrong, and the NEES and the ±3σ coverage are the instruments that say so.

### What to do with a late sample

Messages arrive late. The filter integrates every measurement by its own timestamp, never by when
it arrived, and keeps one time $t_f$. A sample stamped $t$ is handled by comparing it to $t_f$:
inside `max_measurement_delay` (50 ms) behind it, the sample is applied at $t_f$ without
propagation and counted as late; further behind, it is rejected as out of order and counted; ahead,
the filter propagates to it first; and more than `max_dt` (0.5 s) ahead, it does not integrate
across the gap at all, it inflates the covariance instead. The filter keeps no buffer, so a
rejected sample is gone: the policy keeps it causal and its latency bounded.

<LateWheel />

The result is a cliff and not a slope. A sample a few milliseconds past the window is not "a little
worse"; it is *absent*, and a filter that never receives the wheel speed has nothing to say about how far the robot went.
The window is the filter's contract with the middleware, and the way to learn that it has been
broken is the counter in the dashboard, which the node publishes on `/diagnostics`.

## The algorithm

<Algorithm name="ESKF-SE2 (T̂, v̂, b̂, P; ω_m, Δt; z)">

1. propagate: $\omega = \omega_m - \hat b$, $\ \tau = (\hat v\Delta t,\, 0,\, \omega\Delta t)$, $\ \hat T \leftarrow \hat T \bplus \tau$
2. $P \leftarrow F P F^{\T} + Q$, with $F$ and $Q$ as above
3. wheel update: $z = (v_w,\ \omega_w - \omega_g)$; if $|v_w| \le$ `bias_update_max_speed` use both rows of $H$, otherwise only the speed row
4. $S = H P H^{\T} + R$, $\ K = P H^{\T} S^{-1}$, $\ \delta\hat x = K\,(z - h(\hat x))$
5. $P \leftarrow (I - KH)\,P\,(I - KH)^{\T} + KRK^{\T}$
6. inject: $\hat x \leftarrow \hat x \bplus \delta\hat x$; reset: $P \leftarrow G P G^{\T}$
7. return $\hat x$, $P$, and the NIS $\nu = r^{\T} S^{-1} r$

</Algorithm>

Steps 1–2 run at the gyro's rate and steps 3–7 at the wheels'. Between them is the timing rule above.

## Implementation in Rust

### The process model

```rust title="crates/robotics_filters/src/eskf_se2.rs"
    fn predict_inner(
        &mut self,
        rate: f64,
        biased: bool,
        yaw_var_per_s: f64,
        dt: f64,
    ) -> Result<(), FilterError> {
// …
        let omega = if biased {
            rate - self.x.gyro_bias
        } else {
            rate
        };
        let tau = Tangent2::new(self.x.speed * dt, 0.0, omega * dt);
        let mut f = EskfCov::identity();
        f.fixed_view_mut::<3, 3>(0, 0)
            .copy_from(&Se2::exp(&tau).inverse().adjoint());
        f[(idx::PX, idx::V)] = dt;
        if biased {
            f[(idx::TH, idx::B)] = -dt;
        }
        let p = symmetrize(&(f * self.p * f.transpose() + self.noise.discrete(dt, yaw_var_per_s)));
        let p = validate_covariance(&p, &self.tol)?;
        let pose = self.x.pose.boxplus(&tau);
// …
        self.x.pose = pose;
        self.p = p;
        Ok(())
    }
```

`omega` is the gyro rate minus the bias estimate, `tau` is the twist of the step, and `f` is the matrix
$F$ above: an adjoint block, a `dt` from speed to the body-$x$ position error, and a `-dt` from the bias to the
heading, which exists only when the rate came from the gyro. The nominal pose moves by `boxplus`, the
exact step of Chapter 1; the covariance by $F P F^{\T}$ plus the noise of the step. The wheel-only variant
(`biased` false) is what the filter falls back to without a fresh gyro.

### The measurement

```rust title="crates/robotics_filters/src/eskf_se2.rs"
    pub fn update_wheel_with_gyro(
        &mut self,
        wheel_speed: f64,
        wheel_yaw_rate: f64,
        gyro_yaw_rate: f64,
        var_speed: f64,
        var_wheel_yaw_rate: f64,
        var_gyro_yaw_rate: f64,
    ) -> Result<GaussianCorrection<ESKF_DIM, 2>, FilterError> {
// …
        let z = Vector2::new(wheel_speed, wheel_yaw_rate - gyro_yaw_rate);
        let z_hat = Vector2::new(self.x.speed, -self.x.gyro_bias);
        let mut h = SMatrix::<f64, 2, ESKF_DIM>::zeros();
        h[(0, idx::V)] = 1.0;
        h[(1, idx::B)] = -1.0;
        let r = nalgebra::Matrix2::new(var_speed, 0.0, 0.0, var_wheel_yaw_rate + var_gyro_yaw_rate);
        self.update(&(z - z_hat), &h, &r)
    }
```

The measurement is `z = (v_wheel, ω_wheel − ω_gyro)`, the prediction `z_hat = (v, −b)`, and `h` has a `1` in the
speed column and a `−1` in the bias column. Nothing else about the measurement is special: it is handed to the generic
`update`.

### The correction, the injection and the reset

```rust title="crates/robotics_filters/src/eskf_se2.rs"
    pub fn update<const M: usize>(
        &mut self,
        innovation: &SVector<f64, M>,
        h: &SMatrix<f64, M, ESKF_DIM>,
        r: &SMatrix<f64, M, M>,
    ) -> Result<GaussianCorrection<ESKF_DIM, M>, FilterError> {
        let c = joseph_correction(&self.p, h, r, innovation, &self.tol)?;
        let x_new = self.x.boxplus(&c.delta);
// …
        let mut p = c.covariance;
        if self.reset_jacobian {
            let mut g = EskfCov::identity();
            let dxi = Tangent2::new(c.delta[0], c.delta[1], c.delta[2]);
            g.fixed_view_mut::<3, 3>(0, 0)
                .copy_from(&(Matrix3::identity() - se2_ad(&dxi) * 0.5));
            p = validate_covariance(&symmetrize(&(g * p * g.transpose())), &self.tol)?;
        }
        self.x = x_new;
        self.p = p;
        Ok(c)
    }
```

```rust title="crates/robotics_filters/src/update.rs"
pub fn joseph_correction<const N: usize, const M: usize>(
    p: &SMatrix<f64, N, N>,
    h: &SMatrix<f64, M, N>,
    r: &SMatrix<f64, M, M>,
    innovation: &SVector<f64, M>,
    tol: &Tolerances,
) -> Result<GaussianCorrection<N, M>, FilterError> {
// …
    let r = validate_covariance(r, tol)?;
    let s = symmetrize(&(h * p * h.transpose() + r));
    let chol = factor_innovation(&s, tol)?;
    // K = P Hᵀ S⁻¹  ⇔  Kᵀ = S⁻¹ H P  (S symmetric): one Cholesky solve, no explicit inverse.
    let gain = chol.solve(&(h * p)).transpose();
    let delta = gain * innovation;
    let ikh = SMatrix::<f64, N, N>::identity() - gain * h;
    let covariance = symmetrize(&(ikh * p * ikh.transpose() + gain * r * gain.transpose()));
    let covariance = validate_covariance(&covariance, tol)?;
    let nis = innovation.dot(&chol.solve(innovation));
```

`joseph_correction` is the Joseph form of Chapter 1 with one Cholesky solve in place of an inverse; it also returns the
NIS, `innovation.dot(&chol.solve(innovation))`, so the quantity the robot uses to monitor itself costs nothing
extra. `update` then injects the correction with `boxplus`, builds `G = I − ½ ad(δξ)`, and applies `G P Gᵀ`.

### The policy, in two arms

The first arm is the policy of the previous section, written as a guard: a fresh gyro, a gyro average covering the
wheel's interval, and a speed at most `bias_update_max_speed`. Otherwise the update observes the speed alone,
and with no gyro at all the filter counts it as a wheel-only update.

```rust title="crates/robotics_filters/src/consistency.rs"
// …
fn quadratic_form<const N: usize>(
    v: &SVector<f64, N>,
    m: &SMatrix<f64, N, N>,
) -> Result<f64, FilterError> {
// …
    let chol = factor_innovation(m, &Tolerances::default())?;
    Ok(v.dot(&chol.solve(v)))
}
```

```rust title="crates/robotics_filters/src/consistency.rs"
    pub fn report(&self, alpha: f64) -> Result<ConsistencyReport, FilterError> {
        let (lower, upper) = chi2_mean_interval(self.dof, self.n.max(1), alpha)?;
        Ok(ConsistencyReport {
            dof: self.dof,
            samples: self.n,
            mean: self.mean(),
            max: self.max,
            lower,
            upper,
        })
    }
```

```rust title="crates/robotics_core/src/prob/stats.rs"
pub fn chi2_mean_interval(dof: usize, n: usize, alpha: f64) -> Result<(f64, f64), CoreError> {
// …
    let total = dof * n;
    let lo = chi2_quantile(total, 0.5 * alpha)?;
    let hi = chi2_quantile(total, 1.0 - 0.5 * alpha)?;
    Ok((lo / n as f64, hi / n as f64))
}
```

NEES and NIS are the same quadratic form, solved with a Cholesky factor, never an explicit inverse. The interval is the
$\chi^2_{nN}$ quantile pair divided by $n$, as above.

## The rest of the toolbox, on the cart

Two more estimators belong in this chapter's toolbox, and they are worth meeting on Chapter 1's
cart, where every number can be checked by hand. The **information filter** is the Kalman filter written in the
canonical parameters $\Omega = \Sigma^{-1}$ and $\xi = \Sigma^{-1}\mu$, where a measurement *adds* information. The **RTS smoother** runs a backward
pass over the beliefs a filter stored, and every belief improves because it now also knows the future. For the cart ($A = B = C = 1$, $R = 0.25$,
$Q = 0.5$, prior $\mathcal N(0, 1)$, then $u = 1,\ z = 1.7$ and $u = 1,\ z = 2.1$):

```text
information form after predict 1:   Ω̄ = 0.8, ξ̄ = 0.8        (Σ̄ = 1.25, μ̄ = 1)
evidence of z₁ = 1.7:               N(1.7; 1, 1.75) = 0.2622
Kalman filter, t = 2:               μ = 2.280645161290323,  Σ = 0.2741935483870968
information filter, t = 2:          the same, to 1e-12
RTS smoother, t = 1 given t = 2:    μ = 85/62,   Σ = 15/62
RTS smoother, t = 0 given t = 2:    μ = 18.4/62, Σ = 22/62
RTS smoother, t = 2 given t = 2:    equal to the filtered belief
```

The repository pins these in `information::tests::worked_example_1d_cart_agrees_with_kalman` and
`smoother::tests::worked_example_1d_cart`, and this book's checks assert them against the compiled module, the
evidence against its closed form. They are libraries, not nodes: nothing in the robot's graph uses them yet.

What holds in both blocks, and is therefore a statement about the filter and not about a draw of noise: the fused heading is better than
the wheels' on every seed; the raw odometry's NEES is over 100 against an expected 3; the fused filter's is
below 3, conservative, on every seed; and the mean NIS of the wheel-and-gyro update is about 1, under its
expected 2 (the 1-degree-of-freedom speed update has a mean near 0.02 against an expected 1, the conservative wheel noise at
work). What does not: the position of the fused filter varies a lot from seed to seed, 0.108 to 0.317 m
recorded and 0.122 to 0.455 m on the page's draws, because the filter has no absolute position measurement, so
its position error is the random walk of whatever slip and calibration error a draw contains. Do not
read an average into those columns. The repository's own documents say the same: position is not promised
to improve (`docs/STATE_ESTIMATION.md` §4.1).

One more number needs no random stream at all, so it is the same on both sides. With noise-free sensors over 60 s the raw
position RMSE is 1.8·10⁻⁹ m, the fused heading RMSE 2.7·10⁻⁸ rad, and the fused position RMSE 5.7 mm. The last is not
noise: wheel odometry reports the average speed over its last interval, so a step in the commanded speed is seen one wheel sample
late, and the filter lags it by that much (`docs/STATE_ESTIMATION.md` §6).

### What the repository says does not work

A chapter that only shows the wins is half a chapter. The limits recorded in `docs/STATE_ESTIMATION.md`, §6, are these.
There is no absolute measurement, so position drifts with slip and heading with the random walk of the bias; absolute corrections
exist in the library (`update_position`, `update_heading`) and are what the localization of Chapter 4 uses. The filter is planar, with
the gyro assumed aligned with the base. A late sample is rejected and not re-ordered, and the node reports
`DEGRADED` for two seconds after one. And the fused covariance is conservative on purpose, which is
a choice, not a theorem.

<Exercise level="F" difficulty={2} title="The −Δt in F">

Starting from $\omega = \omega_m - \hat b - \delta b$, show that a bias error $\delta b$ moves the heading error by $-\delta b\,\Delta t$ per step
and nothing else in $F$'s last column. Why is the entry for $\delta v$ in the *position* row and not in the heading row?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The true twist is $(v\Delta t, 0, \omega\Delta t)$ with $\omega = \omega_m - b$ and $b = \hat b + \delta b$, so $\delta\tau_\theta = -\delta b\,\Delta t$ and the other
components of $\delta\tau$ do not depend on $\delta b$. A speed error $\delta v$ changes only the translation of the twist along its first axis, $\delta v\,\Delta t$, which is
the body-$x$ position error; heading does not depend on speed in this model.

</details>

</Exercise>

<Exercise level="F" difficulty={3} title="The reset Jacobian to first order">

Derive $G = I - \tfrac12\mathrm{ad}(\hat\delta)$ for the pose block, starting from $\exp(\delta\xi^{+}) = \exp(-\hat\delta)\exp(\delta\xi)$. What
does omitting $G$ assume about the size of $\hat\delta$?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Use BCH, $\log(e^{X}e^{Y}) = X + Y + \tfrac12[X, Y] + \dots$, with $X = -\hat\delta$, $Y = \delta\xi$. Since $[\hat\delta, \hat\delta] = 0$, the bracket
can be taken with the residual $\delta\xi - \hat\delta$, giving $\delta\xi^{+} \approx (I - \tfrac12\mathrm{ad}(\hat\delta))(\delta\xi - \hat\delta)$. Dropping $G$ assumes $\|\mathrm{ad}(\hat\delta)\|\ll 1$:
a correction so small that the second-order term is below the covariance's own accuracy.

</details>

</Exercise>

In `crates/robotics_filters/src/consistency.rs`, add a test that errors drawn from $\mathcal N(0, P)$ pass the interval and errors drawn from
